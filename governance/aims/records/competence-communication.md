# Competence, awareness, resources, and communication

Status: **DRAFT — no competence or resource decision is approved**

## Competence

Before a person independently performs an AIMS role, the accountable executive
or delegated role holder records the role, required knowledge, evidence
reviewed, gaps, development action, evaluator, decision date, and reevaluation
date. Relevant competence may include:

- Agent OS architecture, Event Contracts, tenant isolation, identity,
  capability, approval, effect, completion, recovery, and A2A boundaries;
- AI risk and impact assessment, model limitations, evaluation design,
  affected-party analysis, privacy, security, accessibility, and incident
  response;
- secure Go and web engineering, dependency and build provenance, SQLite
  integrity, Linux operation, and evidence handling;
- applicable legal, contractual, licensing, documentation, audit, and
  certification-claim boundaries; and
- independence, conflicts of interest, evidence sampling, cause analysis, and
  effectiveness review for audit or corrective-action roles.

Repository ownership, credentials, seniority, model output, or prior task
completion is not competence evidence by itself. Gaps require supervised work,
training, independent review, reassignment, or another recorded treatment.

## Awareness

People and automated systems performing in-scope work receive role-appropriate
information about the approved AI policy, their contribution to AIMS
effectiveness, user approval boundaries, foreseeable impacts, incident and
vulnerability reporting, consequences of nonconformity, confidentiality, and
the prohibition on unsupported conformity or certification claims. Awareness
evidence records the audience, content version, delivery date, method, and
confirmation or assessment result.

## Resources

Management review evaluates whether the project has enough competent time,
independent review, security expertise, test and evaluation infrastructure,
provider and supplier oversight, incident response capacity, evidence storage,
backup/recovery capability, accessibility support, qualified advice, and audit
resources for its scope and risks. A missing critical resource blocks the
affected activity rather than being silently accepted.

## Communication plan

| Subject | Audience | Trigger and timing | Owner role | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI policy, scope, and role changes | In-scope role holders and affected maintainers | On approval and before the change operates | AIMS manager | Exact version, audience, time, delivery and acknowledgement |
| Product purpose, limitations, approval boundaries, and material change | Users and operators | Documentation/release review and before affected use | Product owner | Published version and review decision |
| Vulnerabilities and security incidents | Security owner, affected users, suppliers, and authorities where applicable | Per severity, law, contract, and response plan | Incident owner | Authorized notice, recipients, time, content hash, and follow-up |
| Material AI impacts or unsafe behavior | Accountable executive and affected decision owners | Promptly after identification; before continued affected operation | Product owner | Impact record, containment, decision, and communication rationale |
| Supplier or provider change/outage | Technical, supplier, security, and affected user roles | Before planned change or promptly for unplanned events | Supplier owner | Assessment, notice, continuity action, and decision |
| Audit and management-review results | Accountable executive and responsible action owners | At completion and by assigned due dates | AIMS manager | Findings, decisions, action acknowledgement, and closure evidence |
| Public conformity or certification statements | Public and customers | Only after applicable independent evidence and claim review | Accountable executive | Exact authorized claim, scope, validity, reviewer, and publication |

Communications must be accurate, accessible for the intended audience,
appropriately confidential, consistent with incident and legal duties, and
approved when they cross public, legal, sensitive-data, or other consequential
boundaries. Automated systems may draft communications but cannot authorize
them.
